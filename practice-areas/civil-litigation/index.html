Contract and Business Disputes

When an agreement breaks down, the starting point is the contract and the facts: what each party promised, what happened, and what records support the claim or defense. The firm handles breach of contract disputes and business torts, including fraud, misrepresentation, unfair competition, defamation, and tortious interference.

Depending on the agreement and circumstances, a matter may involve damages, specific performance, injunctive relief, or other equitable remedies. For drafting, reviewing, or negotiating an agreement before a dispute arises, see our Delaware business law services.

Preparing to Discuss a Dispute

Organizing the available records can make an initial discussion more useful. Depending on your matter, gather:

  • The agreement, amendments, deeds, surveys, or other documents at issue.
  • Relevant correspondence, notices, invoices, payment records, and photographs.
  • A brief timeline, the names of the parties involved, and any witness information.
  • Any court papers, scheduled hearings, or response dates you have received.

Tell the firm promptly about any pending hearing or response date. Use the office contact information to discuss whether the firm can assist with your matter.

Delaware Civil Litigation Questions

What kinds of civil disputes does the firm handle?

The firm's civil litigation practice includes contract disputes, property and boundary conflicts, business torts, construction matters, neighbor disputes, and personal injury claims. The firm can discuss whether your particular matter fits its practice.

Can a dispute be resolved without going to trial?

Negotiation, mediation, arbitration, or motion practice may resolve a dispute before trial. The available approach depends on the agreement, the parties, the facts, and the procedural posture of the matter.

What should I bring when discussing a contract dispute?

Bring the contract and amendments, relevant correspondence, invoices and payment records, and a short timeline. Include any notices or court papers and identify any dates requiring a response.

Can the firm help with a boundary or easement disagreement?

The firm's existing property litigation practice includes boundary, easement, adverse possession, and neighbor disputes. Deeds, surveys, agreements, photographs, and correspondence can help the attorney evaluate the issue.
